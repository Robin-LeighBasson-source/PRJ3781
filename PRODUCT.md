# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Morrow is for university students and recent graduates, initially framed for a South African audience, as well as employers looking for early-career talent. Candidates may browse between classes on a phone or plan their next move on a laptop. Employers need focused ways to describe roles, discover candidates, and shape graduate hiring experiences.

## Product Purpose

This release is a frontend-only multi-page shell for a future student career network. It demonstrates candidate, employer, job discovery, course, crawler, portfolio, and practical-project experiences without authentication, database connections, application handling, crawler services, analytics, or uploads. Success means every core project module has a believable, navigable interface ready for later integration.

## Positioning

Morrow makes the first step into working life feel clear, current, and worth getting excited about.

## Brand Personality

Ambitious, expressive, and considered. Morrow should give students a sense of forward motion and possibility while feeling more like a premium culture brand than a conventional job board.

## Anti-references

Avoid corporate LinkedIn-blue sameness, generic SaaS card grids, childish campus illustrations, inflated statistics, fake social proof, and close imitation of any reference website. Internshala, WayUp, and Gradlinc inform the information structure; Ocelot Chocolate informs the commitment to art direction, colour, imagery, and restraint.

## Design Principles

- Lead with opportunity discovery, not account creation.
- Make early careers feel aspirational without making false promises.
- Use a confident visual identity instead of default job-board conventions.
- Show believable sample product UI so the future platform feels tangible.
- Treat mobile as a primary experience, not a reduced desktop layout.
- Clearly label mock data and frontend-only interactions so the shell never implies a live service.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

Use semantic HTML, visible keyboard focus, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, and touch-friendly controls as standard frontend practice. Responsive mobile behaviour is required.
